## Biography

Mohammad Alian serves as an Assistant Professor in the ECE Department at Cornell University. He received his Ph.D. and M.S. degrees from UIUC (2020) and UW-Madison (2015), respectively. His research team is focused on redefining the data-delivery hierarchy of future data centers. Mohammad's research has garnered recognition, including best paper nominations at HPCA 2017 and MICRO 2018, an IEEE Micro Top Picks Honorable Mention in 2017, an NSF CAREER award, and induction into the [MICRO Hall of Fame](https://www.sigmicro.org/awards/microhof.php) in 2025. He serves as a co-lead Principal Investigator for the [SRC/DARPA JUMP 2.0 ACE Center for Evolvable Computing](https://acecenter.grainger.illinois.edu/), with the primary objective of revolutionizing distributed computing for the next decade.

I am always looking for motivated students. You can send me an email if you are interested in joining my research group.

## Research

My research interests lie at the intersection of computer architecture and systems, with a focus on redefining the data-delivery hierarchy—memory, storage, and network—of future datacenters. Computation in these datacenters will be distributed over a heterogeneous array of processing elements, packaged modularly within a server's boundaries, making inter- and intra-server data movement the key performance bottleneck. Rather than following the conventional view, in which data delivery revolves around compute, my research treats compute as a paradigm that revolves around the data-delivery hierarchy. To this end, my group seamlessly integrates processor, memory, and network architecture through a co-design with system software to minimize data movement and accelerate large-scale services.
